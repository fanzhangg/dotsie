Waythread Design System

The tokens, components and rules behind Waythread, a phone-first trip planner where friends plan one route together. Everything on this page is the real system, so switch the theme above to see it change.

Less is more

Show information only when it's essential, isn't shown elsewhere, and can't be inferred at a glance.

The plan and the route lead

Stop names are the strongest text. Routes are the only blue. Controls stay quiet.

Built for sharing

Everyone on a trip can edit. People are always visible, and their changes show up as they happen.

Hand off what others do best

Navigation opens in Apple Maps or Google Maps. Waythread never rebuilds it.

Tokens come in three tiers: primitives (--wt-color-*) feed roles (--wt-surface-*, --wt-text-*, --wt-route-* …), and components use only roles and scales. A new theme redefines roles and nothing else. The full guidelines are in design-system/README.md.

Color

Color has a job or it isn't there. Blue means a route or a link. Red means "doesn't fit" or "destructive". A stop's type color marks its marker, on the list and the map. Everything else is a warm neutral. The values below are read live from the current theme.

Primitive palette

Raw values that feed the roles. Components never use these directly.

Type

The phone's own system font, so Waythread feels native everywhere. Each token is a complete font shorthand: font: var(--wt-type-name).

Space and shape

A 4px grid. Phone side margins are --wt-space-4; the list's inner margin is --wt-space-3.

Elevation and motion

Shadows are set by role. A card gets a hairline, the sheet over the map gets a soft lift, and labels and buttons on the map float. Nothing else gets a shadow.

Motion

Sheets and toasts move with --wt-duration-base and --wt-ease-out; color changes use --wt-duration-fast. Every duration drops to 0 when reduced motion is on.

Icons

Line icons on a 24px grid with a 2px stroke and round ends, drawn in the text color. external is only for "opens another app or site". An icon-only button always has an aria-label.

WaythreadIcons.svg('car');           // the size the product uses

Buttons

Labels are verbs that say exactly what happens. A view has at most one primary button.

  • Primary is the one main action: Save, Add, Create. On the trip screen it's Add stop, below the list.
  • Secondary is a quiet action next to content, like Copy beside the trip link.
  • Icon (--icon) is a round button as tall as the others, for an action that sits beside a bigger one, like the + beside Edit stop. Give it an aria-label.
  • Danger always leaves a way back: an Undo toast, or a confirmation when undo isn't possible.
  • Icon buttons only where the icon is universally understood: back, close, edit, home, settings, the day arrows and Directions. Everything else gets words.

People

Each person on a trip gets one identity color, in order, for as long as they're on it. A stack shows who's planning; trip settings lists everyone, by name.

  • With only one person on the trip, show the person-add icon instead of a lone avatar.

Timeline and marker

The heart of the product: one day at a time, down a single rail. A stop row has its marker on the rail, the name and an optional note, and the time on the right. A drive row has a small car on the rail, the drive time as a sentence, and a Directions button that opens the phone's maps app. This is today at 12:05 PM: tap a row to select it.

  • Rows touch. Put them straight inside .wt-tl, with nothing between, so the rail is one solid line. It starts at the first row's marker and ends at the last. Put the timeline on --wt-surface-raised: the marker and car are cut out of the rail in that color.
  • Stop (.wt-tl-stop): marker, name, optional note, time. Nothing else. Set --wt-kind-color on the row; the marker and the ring on the stop happening now both use it. Notes are shown in full, never cut off.
  • Hotel at the start or end of a day is a stop row with an empty .wt-tl-time. Only check-out morning gets a note: Check out.
  • Drive (.wt-tl-drive): 1 hr 49 min drive, and the Directions button. The first drive of a day with no hotel the night before says where it starts (__from).
  • Selected (is-selected on a stop, is-active on a drive) matches a pin or route tapped on the map. It takes the list's one highlight: a grey background, the same for stops and drives, with no outline and no blue. A highlighted drive's car turns darker grey.
  • Directions (__go) is a pale grey circle with a grey icon, the only part of a drive row that looks like a control; it darkens on hover and press. The row itself has no hover or press look.
  • Today: the row happening now gets is-now: the highlight, but only while nothing is selected, with a ring in the stop's type color. Here Cloudcroft is selected, so the drive in progress is a plain row. Rows before it get is-past and dim. Every row on an earlier day is past.
  • Late (is-late) turns the drive time red and adds __late: Arrives 13 min late. That's the only warning.
  • Flash (is-flash) marks a stop a co-editor just changed, once.

Marker

A stop's type icon on its type's color. The same marker sits on the rail, on the map pin and in the day list, so a stop reads the same everywhere. One color per group; none is blue or red.

Markup
<i class="wt-marker" role="img" aria-label="Restaurant" style="--wt-kind-color: var(--wt-kind-food)">${icon('utensils')}</i>
<i class="wt-marker wt-marker--sm" role="img" aria-label="Restaurant" style="--wt-kind-color: var(--wt-kind-food)">${icon('utensils')}</i>
  • Set --wt-kind-color to a --wt-kind-* token, on the marker or on a parent. Without one, the marker is grey (place).
  • --sm (20px) is for the day list. The rail uses the default (30px); pins are 28px, and 36px when selected.
  • The marker has role="img" and an aria-label with the type, so color is never the only signal.

On a phone it's dragged between three heights, like a maps app's sheet: peek, half, and the whole day, with the header docked over it. It never hides. The app sets the height and the system animates it. The day bar steps through the days, and the one add button sits below the list. Try the heights below.

Patterns

How the pieces work together on the trip screen. This is today, Nov 21, at 12:05 PM.

White Sands, NM

10 min drive
34 min drive
58 min driveArrives 13 min late
20 min drive
  1. Map first, plan on top. The map fills the screen; the sheet opens at half height and drags to its top only or nearly full. Home and settings float over the map.
  2. One day at a time. The day bar steps through the days, and the map shows only the open day. During the trip, it opens on today.
  3. Today. The row happening now is highlighted (is-now), unless another row is selected: only one row is highlighted at a time. Rows before it dim (is-past), their routes turn grey, and their pins mute (--muted). The hotel stays bright, since you go back to it tonight.
  4. One look, list and map. A stop's marker is its pin: same icon, same color. The two read as one without numbers.
  5. Tapping links them. A pin selects its stop (is-selected). A route highlights its drive (is-active).
  6. Doesn't fit. A drive longer than the gap between its stops turns its time red and says how late it arrives. No badge.
  7. Hand off. Every drive's Directions button opens Apple Maps or Google Maps. Waythread draws routes but never gives turn-by-turn directions.
  8. Changes from others. A toast names the person and the stop, and the stop flashes once (is-flash).
  9. Deleting. Delete right away and offer Undo. Ask first only when undo isn't possible, like deleting a whole trip.

Accessibility

Text needs 4.5:1 against its background; large text, icons, routes and focus rings need 3:1. These ratios are measured live in the current theme.

ForegroundBackgroundRatioNeeds
  • Focus shows a 2px ring in --wt-border-focus on everything interactive.
  • Touch targets for frequent actions are at least --wt-size-touch (44px) high.
  • Color is never the only signal. A stop's type has an icon and a label as well as a color. A late drive also says Arrives 13 min late. Past rows dim and their pins shrink.
  • Motion drops to nothing when reduced motion is on.
