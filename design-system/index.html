Waythread Design System

The tokens, components and rules behind Waythread, a phone-first trip planner where friends plan one route together. Everything on this page is the real system, so switch the theme above to see it change.

Less is more

Show information only when it's essential, isn't shown elsewhere, and can't be inferred at a glance.

The plan and the route lead

Stop names are the strongest text, and ink and color get quieter as information matters less. Routes are the only blue. Controls stay quiet.

Built for sharing

Everyone on a trip can edit. People are always visible, and their changes show up as they happen.

Hand off what others do best

Navigation opens in Apple Maps or Google Maps. Waythread never rebuilds it.

Tokens come in three tiers: primitives (--wt-color-*) feed roles (--wt-surface-*, --wt-text-*, --wt-route-* …), and components use only roles and scales. A new theme redefines roles and nothing else. The full guidelines are in design-system/README.md.

Color

Color and ink carry information, or they aren't there, and how loud they are says how much it matters. In the plan, loudest first:

ColorMeansWhere
RedNeeds fixingA drive that doesn't fit: its time, its car, Arrives 13 min late. Destructive buttons.
BlackNow, and what you read firstThe row happening now: a black ring on its marker or a black car, and its time in primary ink. Stop names. The one primary button.
Type colorsWhat kind of placeMarkers and pins only. One color per type, all about as light, so no type outranks another.
GreysRead next, then detailTimes and notes in secondary ink, drives and detail in tertiary.
Light greyOverPast rows' text, markers and cars, past routes, muted pins. The color drains out of what's done.
BlueRoutes and linksRoute lines on the map, links in notes. Never a highlight.

The greys are one warm ramp, stepped by lightness: fine steps at both ends for surfaces and lines, wide ones in the middle, where each step is an ink with its own rank (primary 18:1, secondary 7:1, tertiary 5:1, past 2.6:1 on white). A highlighted row is grey, never tinted. The values below are read live from the current theme.

Elevation and motion

Shadows are set by role. A card gets a hairline, the sheet over the map gets a soft lift, and labels and buttons on the map float. Nothing else gets a shadow.

Motion

Sheets and toasts move with --wt-duration-base and --wt-ease-out; color changes use --wt-duration-fast. Every duration drops to 0 when reduced motion is on.

Buttons that change

When what you can do changes, a row of buttons morphs into the next set instead of swapping, on --wt-duration-base. A button in both sets moves and stretches to its new place, its old look fading out over it: selecting a stop shrinks Add stop to the right into the +. A new button grows in, a wide one from its left edge; one that's gone fades out underneath. Open in Maps and Directions stretch into each other. Pick what's selected:

Icons

Line icons on a 24px grid with a 2px stroke and round ends, drawn in the text color. external is only for "opens another app or site". An icon-only button always has an aria-label.

WaythreadIcons.svg('car');           // the size the product uses

Buttons

Labels are verbs that say exactly what happens. A view has at most one primary button.

  • Primary is the one main action: Save, Add, Create. On the trip screen it's Add stop, below the list.
  • Secondary is every other action, white with a hairline outline: Copy, Make a new link, Save for offline, Sign out. There are no text-only buttons.
  • Icon (--icon) is a round button as tall as the others, for an action that sits beside a bigger one, like Edit stop and the + beside Open in Maps. Give it an aria-label.
  • Danger is the secondary button with a red label, for an action that destroys something: Delete trip. It always leaves a way back: an Undo toast, or a confirmation when undo isn't possible.
  • Icon buttons only where the icon is universally understood: back, close, edit, home, settings, the day arrows and Directions. Everything else gets words.

People

Each person on a trip gets one identity color, in order, for as long as they're on it. A stack shows who's planning; trip settings lists everyone, by name.

  • With only one person on the trip, show the person-add icon instead of a lone avatar.
  • A Google photo covers the initial when there is one. Keep the initial and color underneath: they show while it loads, and if it fails.

Settings sections

Trip settings and Account are a column of sections, one thing each, divided by hairlines. Every section has the same order: its title, a line on what it does, its controls, then a hint or a quiet text button.

  • Rows touch. Put them straight inside .wt-tl, with nothing between, so the rail is one solid line. It starts at the first row's marker and ends at the last. Put the timeline on --wt-surface-raised: the marker and car are cut out of the rail in that color.
  • Stop (.wt-tl-stop): marker, name, optional note, time. Nothing else. Set --wt-kind-color on the row and the marker. Notes are shown in full, never cut off.
  • Hotel at the start or end of a day is a stop row with an empty .wt-tl-time. Only check-out morning gets a note: Check out.
  • Drive (.wt-tl-drive): 1 hr 49 min drive, and the Directions button. The first drive of a day with no hotel the night before says where it starts (__from).
  • Selected (is-selected on a stop, is-active on a drive) matches a pin or route tapped on the map. It takes the list's one highlight: a grey background, the same for stops and drives, with no outline and no blue. A selected drive's car turns darker grey.
  • Directions (__go) is a pale grey circle with a grey icon, the only part of a drive row that looks like a control; it darkens on hover and press. The row itself has no hover or press look.
  • Today: the row happening now gets is-now and the blackest ink: a black ring on a stop's marker, a black car on a drive, and its time in primary ink. While nothing is selected it's also the highlighted row. Here Cloudcroft is selected, so the drive in progress keeps its black car on a plain row. Rows before it get is-past: their text turns the past ink and their markers and cars turn grey. Every row on an earlier day is past.
  • Late (is-late) turns the drive time and its car red and adds __late: Arrives 13 min late. Red outranks everything, so a late drive happening now is still red. Once it's over it turns grey like the rest.
  • Flash (is-flash) marks a stop a co-editor just changed, once.

Marker

A stop's type icon on its type's color. The same marker sits on the rail, on the map pin and in the day list, so a stop reads the same everywhere. Each type has its own color, following maps apps where it can, all about as light, so a type tells you what a place is and never that it matters more. None is blue (routes) or red (doesn't fit).

Markup
<i class="wt-marker" role="img" aria-label="Restaurant" style="--wt-kind-color: var(--wt-kind-restaurant)">${icon('utensils')}</i>
<i class="wt-marker wt-marker--sm" role="img" aria-label="Restaurant" style="--wt-kind-color: var(--wt-kind-restaurant)">${icon('utensils')}</i>
  • Set --wt-kind-color to a --wt-kind-* token, on the marker or on a parent. Without one, the marker is grey (place).
  • --sm (20px) is for the day list. The rail uses the default (30px); pins are 28px, and 36px when selected.
  • The marker has role="img" and an aria-label with the type, so color is never the only signal.

All days

The overview, before the first day: every day under its name (.wt-tl-day), with its stops and their times. Tap a day's name to open it.

  • Day headings sit in the timeline, between the days' stops, ending in View day (.wt-tl-day__open) in words rather than a forward icon, so it isn't one more arrow under the day bar's: tapping one opens that day. They're a label, not a name: small grey capitals (--wt-type-overline), starting at the list's left edge, where the markers start, so a date never reads as a stop. They're not on the rail, so each day's rail runs from its first stop to its last.
  • Stops only. Drives and notes stay on the day's own page; a hotel keeps its nights, and check-out morning its Check out. Selecting, is-now and is-past work as on a day. A past day's heading dims with its stops (is-past).

Folders

Stops that follow each other can share a folder: a name that groups them, like the viewpoints in a park. A folder isn't a place. Closed, it's one row: its stops' marker with a second disc piled behind it, its name, what's in it, and when it starts. Open, its stops and the drives between them hang from its name, like files under a folder. Swipe a stop right to put it in the folder above it, or in a new one; left to take it out.

Modal sheet and day list

For editing one thing. It slides up from the bottom; a tap on the backdrop or Escape closes it. Destructive actions sit on the left, the main action on the right.

Edit stop

Day list

Tapping the day in the day bar opens every day of the trip in a modal sheet, with the markers of each day's stops and the hotel you sleep at, so you can see what each day holds. Tap a day to try it.

  • aria-current="date" marks the open day. Picking a day closes the sheet and opens that day.
  • Show up to six markers: the day's stops in order, then the hotel. A place that appears twice shows once.

Toast

News at the top of the screen: a confirmation, a co-editor's change, an undo, a failure. It stays as long as it takes to read (3 to 8 seconds, at least 5 with an action), holds while you point at it, and goes with a tap or a swipe up. One at a time, and news waits behind Undo. A notice that needs a second line is rich: an icon, a heading and a detail line, on a card. An error is rich, with its icon in red.

Sam updated Cloudcroft
  • Name the person and the stop: Sam added Oliver Lee Memorial State Park. Several changes at once: Sam updated Cloudcroft + 2 more.
  • An error's heading says what failed, its detail what to do: Couldn’t save your change · Check your connection, then try again. Never “Error” or “Something went wrong”.

Dialog

A question before an action that can't be undone, or that other people will notice. Small, in the middle, over everything, so a sheet can ask without closing. The title asks it, a line says what happens, and the buttons are Cancel and one that names the action. When it destroys something the action is red and Cancel has the focus.

Delete “White Sands, NM”?

It’s deleted for everyone on the trip. You can undo right after.

  • The action names what happens: Delete trip, Make new link, never OK.
  • Ask only when there's no undo, or the change reaches others. Deleting a stop doesn't ask: it has Undo.

Map pins, routes and buttons

A pin is a round dot in the stop's type color, centered on the place, with the stop's number inside; the hotel at either end of a day has a smaller dot inside. Where pins in view crowd (zoomed out, or places close together) they're all small dots with no number, so places and routes stay visible; zoom in and every number comes back at once. A selected pin grows; a muted one (over, or off the route on show) shrinks and turns grey. The map carries no text: names and times are in the list. It shows only the open day. Routes are the only blue on screen, and turn grey once they're over. Map libraries draw with script, so they read the route tokens at draw time.

  1. Map first, plan on top. The map fills the screen; the sheet opens at half height and drags to its top only or nearly full. Home and settings float over the map.
  2. One day at a time. The day bar steps through the days, and the map shows only the open day. During the trip, it opens on today.
  3. Today. The row happening now is highlighted (is-now), unless another row is selected: only one row is highlighted at a time. Rows before it dim (is-past), their routes turn grey, and their pins mute (--muted). The hotel stays bright, since you go back to it tonight.
  4. One look, list and map. A stop's marker is its pin: same icon, same color. The two read as one without numbers.
  5. Tapping links them. A pin selects its stop (is-selected). A route highlights its drive (is-active).
  6. Doesn't fit. A drive longer than the gap between its stops turns its time red and says how late it arrives. No badge.
  7. Hand off. Every drive's Directions button opens Apple Maps or Google Maps. Waythread draws routes but never gives turn-by-turn directions.
  8. Changes from others. A toast names the person and the stop, and the stop flashes once (is-flash).
  9. Deleting. Delete right away and offer Undo. Ask first only when undo isn't possible, like deleting a whole trip.

Accessibility

Text needs 4.5:1 against its background; large text, icons, routes and focus rings need 3:1. These ratios are measured live in the current theme.

ForegroundBackgroundRatioNeeds
  • Focus shows a 2px ring in --wt-border-focus on everything interactive.
  • Touch targets for frequent actions are at least --wt-size-touch (44px) high.
  • Color is never the only signal. A stop's type has an icon and a label as well as a color. A late drive also says Arrives 13 min late. Past rows dim and their pins shrink.
  • Motion drops to nothing when reduced motion is on.
